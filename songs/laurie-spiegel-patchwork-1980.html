1980
Patchwork
Laurie Spiegel

Spiegel wrote her own software to compose. Working at Bell Labs on the GROOVE system, she composed algorithmically - computer processes generating musical decisions. She believed technology could free music from the constraints of human performance.

Rules generate forms; forms recombine. Six frequency bands, translated into shape and luminosity, modulate continuously. The image follows the same logic the music was built with - process as pattern.

Algorithmic composition as a way of discovering what music wants to be. The computer becomes a collaborator - generating structural possibilities that exceed what any single intention could anticipate.

Spiegel developed Music Mouse, a software instrument that translated simple input into complex musical output. The tool embodied her belief that technology should expand human musical possibility.

Her work at Bell Labs brought her into contact with researchers across disciplines. She describes her compositional approach as empirical - trying things, listening, adjusting, trying again.

Patchwork is the sound of a system discovering its own possibilities. The composer sets conditions and then follows where they lead - a collaboration between human intention and algorithmic process.
